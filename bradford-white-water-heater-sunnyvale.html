Step-by-Step Operational Workflow

  1. Identify the customer objective.

    Determine whether the customer requires repair, replacement, installation planning, maintenance, efficiency improvement, or emergency assistance. The purpose of the project determines which information is most important.

  2. Document the existing water-heating system.

    Record manufacturer, model, serial number, fuel type, capacity, age, installation location, visible condition, and known service history. This establishes the current system baseline.

  3. Evaluate property demand.

    Review household or business requirements, including occupants, bathrooms, appliances, operating hours, simultaneous demand, and recovery expectations. Equipment selection should match actual usage patterns.

  4. Review equipment suitability.

    Assess whether the proposed Bradford White model matches the required capacity, fuel source, available utilities, physical space, and installation environment.

  5. Analyze installation requirements.

    Evaluate plumbing connections, gas or electrical requirements, venting, combustion air, drainage, pressure conditions, seismic restraint, clearances, and maintenance access.

  6. Create transparent project documentation.

    Prepare a clear scope identifying equipment, installation tasks, required modifications, maintenance expectations, warranty information, and possible project variables.

  7. Complete installation verification.

    Confirm that the installed equipment matches the approved documentation. Review connections, safety components, startup operation, temperature settings, leak checks, and required inspections.

  8. Provide customer education.

    Explain system operation, maintenance requirements, shutoff locations, warranty information, and warning signs that may require future service.

  9. Monitor performance and update documentation.

    Maintain records of service history, customer feedback, equipment performance observations, and future maintenance needs.
